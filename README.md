# Garden of Delight

A compact walled English cottage garden (about 54 × 86 m inside its brick wall) lit for a night exhibition — fairy-lit trees, fields of glowing stalks, frosted globes, a tunnel of light arches — with ten black holes of different sizes hanging in it: pinholes nesting in tree canopies that drain a single bush, larger ones over the walks, and a giant hanging about 16 m above the great fountain that you can walk beneath.

Each void sleeps as a small dark bead. Walk close enough and it wakes (bigger voids notice you from farther away — ~10 m for a pinhole, ~28 m for the giant): it grows, lenses the scene behind it, twists and pulls nearby lights toward its horizon, drains the light from the ground, trees and globes around it, and draws streams of stolen light spiralling in. It also tugs on you. Walk away and the light seeps back over the next half-minute.

**Play:** open `index.html` (or the GitHub Pages site).

- Desktop: WASD / arrows walk, mouse looks (click to capture, Esc to release), Shift hurries, **E** opens a new void of random size ahead of you, **M** toggles sound. The **pull** slider sets how hard the voids tug on you (0–150%). **F** shows the frame rate and render resolution.
- Time of day: the pill at bottom right toggles **night** (default) ↔ **live** (the real sky right now: your clock and today's date; also **T**), and its slider scrubs to any hour on the garden's own calendar date. The sun is placed by a solar-position calculation (≈42°N, longitude from your time zone), with day sky, clouds, twilight and stars. In daylight the voids carve pockets of night out of the day.
- Touch: drag the left half to walk, the right half to look, double-tap (or the button) to open a void.
- **Look down** and you are there: a future astronaut in a hard-shell suit, cel-shaded with ink outlines. It walks and runs with you, reaches out when you click a void, and casts your shadow, helmet and pack included.

## The great void

A giant black hole hangs 12 m above the grand fountain. It never fully sleeps; walk within ~40 m and it wakes, draining the central garden while an accretion disk of stolen light orbits it (inner edge white-hot, outer amber, the approaching side brighter). The lensing pass bends the far side of the disk over the top of the shadow and lets the near side cross in front; the disk's glow lights the fountain and parterre below, and the whole thing hangs reflected in the pool.

## Time

Every plant, tree and patch of ground cover has its own clock. A static clock at distance r from a void ticks at √(1 − Σ rₛ/r) of the garden's calendar rate (Schwarzschild time dilation, weak-field potentials summed over all voids; rₛ = 2.6 × the void's horizon radius, so the effect grows as a void wakes and feeds). Each frame, each plant integrates its own proper time and its phenology is read from that, not from the calendar: near an awake void the border lags behind the season, and under the giant the parterre can still be in bloom while the outer garden has gone bare.

You have a clock too. Your watch is real time; the garden's calendar advances at 1/√(1 − Σ rₛ/r) of that while you stand in a well, so the rest of the garden runs fast for you, its light and the sky arrive blueshifted, the music rises in pitch, and the HUD tallies how much younger than the garden you've become. Light from deeper wells than yours arrives redshifted.

**Seasons:** S cycles paused / 1 day / 1 week / 1 month per second; the slider scrubs the calendar (which also sets the sun's height); R replants so you can watch three years of establishment. Phenology comes from Shortwood Meadow's sourced bloom months: spring emergence (early and late species), bloom windows, summer-dormant alliums and poppies, standing seed heads, grasses that stand as straw until cut back in February, evergreen lavender, pinks and lamb's ears, woody roses and hydrangea, deciduous trees with spring flush, autumn colour and leaf fall, and a lawn that greens, dries and dulls through the year.

## The garden

Small on purpose, so every surface can be close-up quality. Materials are photo-scans; stonework is modelled in Blender; plants are procedural structure dressed in scanned leaves.

- **Layout:** an iron gate in a brick wall; a gravel forecourt; a tunnel of light arches through double herbaceous borders backed by yew; a canal garden with arching jets, yew columns, box hedges and lavender walks; a round central garden with a three-tier fountain, urns and box-edged parterre beds inside a yew ring; a loop walk with deep borders against the ivy-patched wall; two bowl fountains, rose arches, a bench niche, two long-grass meadows, six trees, ten cast-iron lamp posts.
- **Planting design:** a clipped English frame (box balls on a seven-metre beat, yew cones and rose obelisks behind, topiary lawns, standards, box-edged parterre) holding loose, layered matrix planting inside it, after Oudolf & Kingsbury, Rainer & West and Dunnett. Every bed is built in four layers: **structure** (about 10%: long-lived plants with strong form in odd-numbered groups on a steady beat, every third group stepping forward), **theme** (about 25%: the showy seasonal plants in long, thin, diagonal drifts that recur along the bed), **scatter** (about 5–10%: see-through emergents such as Verbena bonariensis, drumstick alliums and Knautia, dotted singly, front included, so no bed is a height staircase) and **matrix** (the rest: a low, knitting ground layer, mostly a grass, planted tight so no soil shows). Palettes follow aspect: a Beth Chatto-style dry garden of silvers and blues against the sun-baked north wall; a hot late-summer prairie (Sporobolus matrix) facing the evening sun on the east; a cool Oudolf mix of blues, plums and pinks on the west; a green-and-white woodland edge (Hakonechloa, Astrantia, Japanese anemones, hydrangeas) in the shadow of the south wall. The entrance double border is mirrored across the walk, with Calamagrostis standing in pairs like a colonnade. The parterre is lavender-edged, with roses on a regular beat through a matrix of catmint and lady's mantle and alliums rising through. The two long-grass areas are prairie meadows of Sporobolus and Schizachyrium with coneflowers, rudbeckias, liatris and Knautia. Grasses and seed heads stand through winter. Ten plants were added for this (Sesleria, Deschampsia, Hakonechloa, Molinia, Verbena bonariensis, Sanguisorba, Persicaria, Knautia, Astrantia, Allium sphaerocephalon).
- **Ground:** four scanned PBR materials (lawn, mulch, gravel, granite setts: colour, normal and AO/roughness maps) blended by two painted masks, with noise-broken edges, setts edging along every path, painted contact shadows under everything, and wet stone round the fountains. Gravel is pale and compacted down the trodden middle of each path and darker, mossier and littered toward its edges.
- **Lawn:** ~360k GPU grass blades in two tiers that follow the walker — curved nine-vertex blades with rounded normals, clumping, mowing stripes, and long golden grass in the meadows.
- **Stonework (Blender):** fountains, pool kerbs of jointed coping stones, urns and pier finials are built by `tools/blender/build_stone.py` — turned profiles carved with gadroons, flutes and pinecone scales, weathered, simplified, with ambient occlusion baked into vertex colours. `blender/stone.blend` opens in Blender. In the garden they wear a scanned limestone, projected triplanar, with moss on upward faces, rain streaks and a wet variant.
- **Plants:** 57 perennials, bulbs, grasses, shrubs and roses from [Shortwood Meadow](https://github.com/samuelling/shortwood-meadow) (`src/plantart.js`, `src/looks.js`) — plant structure and painted flowers — now with scanned leaves (colour + normal maps) for every leaf type. ~3,000 plants draw as instanced meshes with distance LOD and wind sway.
- **Showpiece flowers in true 3D** (`src/flowers3d.js`): English-rose rosettes, double peonies, crinkled poppies, allium globes, delphinium, lupine and foxglove spires built floret by floret.
- **Hedges & topiary:** swept, clipped forms shelled with sprigs, some growing out of the face and some lying against it. The yew sprig is baked in Blender (`tools/blender/bake_yew.py`): a fan of sprays modelled needle by needle, rendered from above with Cycles for colour and for a normal map. Box uses scanned twigs.
- **Trees (Blender):** `tools/blender/build_trees.py` grows three variants as a skeleton of trunk, scaffold limbs, branches and twigs reaching for the crown's surface. Trunk, root buttresses and the base of each limb are fused by a voxel remesh, so limbs flow out of the trunk and roots flare into the ground. About 1,700 boughs of scanned lime leaves sit on the twigs of each tree, and the sky each bough (and each patch of bark) can see is ray-cast through the crown and stored as vertex colour, so canopies are bright outside and dim within. The garden instances them, scaled to each tree's crown, with bark projected triplanar and leaf normals leaned outward so a crown lights as one rounded mass. `blender/trees.blend` opens in Blender.
- **Props:** scanned lamp posts, gate, bench and a stone cat on the pool rim; coping stones (laid end to end along the wall and canal, each its own tone) and path lanterns modelled in Blender (`tools/blender/build_props.py`).
- **You (Blender):** `tools/blender/build_astronaut.py` models the suit the way a hard suit is built: rigid shells joined by bearing rings, a dark ball at every joint, chest unit, wrist display, life-support pack, helmet. Fifteen segments, each with its origin at its joint, so `src/body.js` poses it by turning whole segments: legs by two-bone IK to feet that stay planted through each stride, arms swinging against them, cadence and stride from how fast you actually moved. Shading is cel: light arrives in flat bands (sun or moon with a hard-edged shadow, the exhibit's glow, sky, a small suit lamp), compressed so the suit never blows out, and each shell is drawn again inside-out and swollen along smoothed normals for an ink line of constant width on screen. The voids drain it like everything else; the instruments keep glowing. You never see your own helmet, but your shadow has it.
- **Water:** one planar reflection pass shared by every pool and the canal (the voids appear in it as black spheres), a refracted stone floor with caustics, ripples, ribbons of falling water, foam and droplet jets.
- **Light:** sun or moon with soft shadows following you, a sky with drifting cloud, environment lighting, fog; the exhibit's light pools and the voids' drain are injected into every material.
- **Rendering:** 4× multisampling with alpha-to-coverage (smooth leaf edges without sorting), screen-space ambient occlusion from the depth buffer, bloom, a light colour grade, ACES tone mapping. Resolution drops by itself if frames run slow; `?q=low`, `?msaa=0` and `?ao=0` turn things down by hand.

## Settings

**O** (or the *settings* button) opens a drawer with four tabs. Everything in it is remembered between visits.

- **Garden:** season speed (paused, real time, up to two months a second), *make it now*, day of the year, garden age (time since planting), whether each plant keeps its own proper time near the voids, replant, the weather (live or by hand: rain, snow, snow lying, wind, gusts), and whether you have a body.
- **Voids:** pull on you, exact or ×7 clocks, size, how much light they eat, how far away they notice you, lensing, the great void's disk, the streams of stolen light, evaporation time, whether the garden's voids re-form, and buttons to open a void or evaporate the nearest or all of them.
- **Light:** the place under the sky (latitude and longitude, *use my location*, or back to Watertown, MA), the faintest stars shown, moon size, sky mode (night, live, set) and hour, exhibit lights, moonlight, exposure, glow, haze, cloud, ambient occlusion and render resolution.
- **Hands:** Invisible Hands. Start or join a room and another app, or the console on a phone (https://samuelling.github.io/InvisibleHands/console/), can move 20 of the controls (season speed, day of the year, garden age, wind, gusts, pull, exact clocks, void size, light eaten, lensing, disk, streams, open a void, evaporate the nearest, hour, exhibit lights, moonlight, exposure, glow, cloud), each marked with the Invisible Hands tag. The garden publishes four readings back: the light where you stand, your clock rate, how near a void is, and how awake the great void is. Your location is never shared.

## Weather

By default the garden has the weather that the place under the sky is having now (Light tab; Watertown, MA unless you change it): cloud cover, wind speed, direction and gustiness, rain, snow, fog, and snow already lying. Conditions come from [Open-Meteo](https://open-meteo.com) (free, no key), asked again every ten minutes. Only the latitude and longitude are sent, rounded to two decimals (about a kilometre).

- **Rain and snow** fall in a box that travels with you. Each streak and flake is lit by the light where it is, so rain glints in the exhibit's colours, goes dark inside a void's reach and bends toward the horizon on its way down. The wind slants it.
- **Overcast** is a solid grey deck that hides the sun, moon and stars and cuts the direct light; fog, rain and snow thicken the haze.
- **Snow settles** on whatever faces the sky (lawn, paths, hedge tops, stone, leaves), patchily at first, and melts when it stops. **Rain** darkens and glosses the ground, which dries slowly.
- **Sound:** rain hiss and a low wind that rises with the gusts.
- **By hand:** Garden tab → Weather. Moving Rain, Snow, Snow lying, Wind, Gusts or Cloud takes over from the live weather, starting from what it is at that moment; *local, live* hands it back.
- **Where it works:** a page hosted on claude.ai may not call outside services, so there the live weather is unavailable and the sliders are used; the GitHub Pages copy (or any ordinary web host) can reach it. No thunder or lightning yet.

**Make it now** (Garden tab) sets today's date, a real-time calendar, the live sky and the live weather in one press. *Real time* on its own runs the seasons at one day per day; the season button on the main controls steps through it too.

## The sky

The sky is the real one over the chosen place (Watertown, MA by default; north is toward the bench, south toward the gate).

- **Sun** by a standard solar-position formula, with refraction near the horizon.
- **Moon** by a truncated Meeus lunar series with parallax: position to about 0.2°, at its true size (half a degree; the *moon size* setting enlarges it), lit by the real sun direction so its phase, terminator and tilt are right, with faint earthshine. Moonlight on the garden follows its phase and height: new-moon nights are darker.
- **Stars:** 5,044 Hipparcos stars to magnitude 6 with their true colours (from B–V), precessed to the date, dimmed and set twinkling more near the horizon. The *faintest stars* setting stands in for light pollution: around magnitude 3.5–4 under Watertown's real sky.
- **Planets:** Mercury, Venus, Mars, Jupiter and Saturn from JPL's approximate orbital elements, with their brightness.
- **Milky Way** along the true galactic plane, brightest toward Sagittarius.

Checked against PyEphem: stars to 0.01°, planets to about 0.05°, the moon to about 0.2° and its phase to 0.1%. In *live* mode it is the sky right now. In *night* and *set* modes it is the sky for the garden's calendar date at that hour, in your browser's time zone.

## How it works

- **Drain field:** every light, surface and ground fragment computes `L = Π (1 − sᵢ·fᵢ(d))` over up to 12 voids, where `s` is each void's (slowly decaying) strength and `f` a smooth falloff whose reach grows as it wakes.
- **Infall:** light points inside a void's reach are rotated around its axis and pulled toward it in the vertex shader; a separate GPU particle system spirals light from the surrounding ground into the horizon, tinted by the local light colours and blue-shifting as it falls.
- **Lensing (exact Schwarzschild optics):** each void's black disc is its shadow, so its critical impact parameter is the disc radius and its horizon is `rs = R / 2.598`. For every pixel the post pass finds the ray's impact parameter `b` and bends it by the true Schwarzschild angle `α(b)`, tabulated at load by integrating the null geodesic (it reduces to `2rs/b` far out and diverges logarithmically at the photon sphere, which is what wraps the garden's lights into arcs and a photon ring). Sources close behind a void are displaced less (thin-lens factor `D_ls/D_s`); things in front are untouched. Rays bent out of the frame sample a cube map rendered round the walker, one face every other frame while a void is awake (`?env=0` turns it off).
- **The giant's disk:** ray-traced in the same pass. In the plane of each ray, `u = 1/r` obeys `u'' + u = (3/2) rs u²`; the shader steps it round the hole and catches each crossing of the disk plane, so the far side of the disk arches over and under the shadow. The disk runs from the innermost stable orbit (3 rs) outward with a thin-disk brightness profile, orbital Doppler beaming and gravitational redshift.
- **Clocks:** a static clock at distance `r` ticks at `sqrt(1 − rs/r)`. The **clocks** button switches between *exact* (the horizon the optics imply: time slows a few percent at most) and *×7* (each horizon enlarged about sevenfold so the slowing can be felt; the default).
- **Evaporation (click a void):** clicking a void (or tapping it on a phone) makes it evaporate by Hawking radiation, sped up enormously. A black hole radiates like a body at a temperature inversely proportional to its mass, and loses mass at a rate proportional to 1/M², so M³ falls linearly: M(t) = M₀(1 − t/τ)^(1/3). It shrinks slowly, then all at once, glowing hotter and brighter (red, then white, then blue-white) as it goes, while the light it took seeps back, and it ends in a flash. A real hole this size would take about 10^57 years, and since it is colder than the cosmic microwave background it would in fact be growing. Voids you opened are gone for good; the garden's own re-form after 45 seconds (the great void after 75).
- **What is not physics:** the drain of light and the pull on the walker are the piece's fiction. A real hole this size would weigh about 150 Earths.
- **Ground light:** pools of light are baked into a canvas light map at load and multiplied by the drain field per fragment.
- **Sound (Web Audio):** a chord pad whose filter and level follow the light where you stand, glints that fade as the light does, a sub-rumble near awake voids, and an inhale when one wakes.

ES modules, no build step; three.js r160 from jsDelivr; about 19 MB of textures and models in `assets/`.
